# Player evaluation framework

Use only dimensions that can change the decision.

## This matchday

1. **Vote probability / expected minutes** — a likely 80-90 minute starter usually beats a marginally higher-upside rotation risk.
2. **Role quality** — central attacking role, advanced positioning, penalty duty and set pieces can materially increase upside.
3. **Matchup** — opponent defensive strength, home/away, expected possession and likely game state.
4. **Recent underlying involvement** — minutes, shots, shots in box, xG, chances created/xA, touches in dangerous zones where available.
5. **Fantasy floor/ceiling** — defenders and midfielders may contribute differently depending on scoring and modifier rules.
6. **News volatility** — late fitness tests and tactical rotation reduce confidence.

## Defenders and modifier

When a defensive modifier exists, evaluate the unit rather than four isolated defenders:

- probability of a clean or low-concession match;
- individual vote reliability;
- likelihood of cards/errors/rotation;
- attacking upside as a secondary benefit;
- goalkeeper matchup when the modifier formula depends on goalkeeper/defender average.

Do not sacrifice a strong structural modifier edge for a tiny attacking-upside difference unless the league rules justify it.

## Medium-term / season-long

Add:

- role stability and competition for minutes;
- team attacking/defensive quality;
- set-piece hierarchy;
- injury and suspension risk;
- schedule density and European rotation;
- sustainable per-90 production rather than only recent fantasy points;
- acquisition price, replacement level and roster construction.

## Confidence split

When comparing two options, a rough split is useful:

- `70/30+`: clear preference;
- `60/40`: real edge but plausible alternative;
- `55/45`: essentially a toss-up, explain the tie-breaker.

Do not use a numeric split when source uncertainty is too high to justify it.
